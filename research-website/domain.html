01 — Literature survey

Review of related literature & prior studies

A critical evaluation of existing computational methodologies and empirical literature across each research domain.

01 Member 1

University degree recommendation

Conventional advisory relies on human counsellors or interest inventories such as Holland's RIASEC. These describe broad vocational types but ignore competitive admission cut-offs, district quotas and stream-specific requirements set by the University Grants Commission (UGC) in Sri Lanka.

Recent studies apply Random Forest, SVM and Naive Bayes to map grades to programmes, yet forward classifiers overlook students who already have a target career. This motivates a hybrid design combining backward-chaining rules with a calibrated Random Forest trained on national A/L cut-off data.

02 J A Wickremathilaka

Skill extraction and labour-market alignment

Job recommendation research largely uses Bag-of-Words or plain cosine similarity, which causes “frequency flattening”: common keywords such as Git, SQL or Agile drown out discriminative skills like PyTorch, FastAPI or Terraform.

Single-model classifiers also show high variance on multi-label skill data. A TF-IDF weighted stacking ensemble (Random Forest, Linear SVC and Multinomial Naive Bayes into a Logistic Regression meta-learner), paired with a Neo4j knowledge graph, enables a quantified Skill Gap Index (SGI) and the discovery of non-linear career pivots.

03 Member 3

Wearable telemetry and student stress

Stress is usually measured through retrospective questionnaires such as PSS-10 and DASS-21, which are subjective, episodic and unable to support real-time intervention.

Biomedical IoT research shows autonomic responses can be tracked non-invasively from wearable sensors. Using PPG, heart rate variability (SDNN, RMSSD, LF/HF), accelerometer activity and skin temperature, machine learning models can classify stress into discrete levels and prompt personalised relaxation strategies.

04 Member 4

Mentorship matching and credential verification

Industry mentorship improves graduate employability, yet university mentoring programmes remain manual and prone to mismatches. Collaborative filtering approaches suffer from the cold-start problem when interaction history is limited.

Semantic text embeddings (e.g. Sentence-BERT) over CVs and student goal statements avoid this dependency, while automated document parsing and institutional verification prevent fraudulent mentor profiles.

02 — Research gap

Identified research gaps in existing literature

A

Siloed advisory domains

Degree choice, skill readiness, wellbeing and mentorship are handled as separate processes rather than one continuous journey.

B

Curriculum–industry mismatch

Static curricula lag behind industry stacks, and graduates have no quantified measure of how far they are from a target role.

C

No career pivot modelling

Existing tools suggest a single destination and do not show which adjacent skills open alternative pathways.

D

Unmonitored academic stress

There is no continuous physiological monitoring to detect exhaustion before it affects performance.

E

Unverified mentorship networks

Mentor matching lacks semantic compatibility scoring backed by verified credentials.

03 — Research problem

How can a student be guided from examination results to a suitable degree, have their skill gaps measured against real industry demand, have their wellbeing monitored, and be connected with verified mentors — within one unified decision-support system?

04 — Objectives

Primary & individual research objectives

To design, build, evaluate and deploy an integrated AI and knowledge graph ecosystem providing personalised guidance across degree selection, skill gap analysis, stress intervention and verified mentorship.

Objective 01 · Member 1

Degree recommendation

Build a dual-tier system combining backward-chaining rules and Random Forest classification to predict degree pathways from A/L Z-scores, district quotas and interests.

Objective 02 · J A Wickremathilaka

Career pathway & Skill Gap Index

Develop a TF-IDF weighted multi-label stacking ensemble, an academic affinity resolver and a Neo4j knowledge graph to compute the SGI and map career pivots.

Objective 03 · Member 3

Stress & wellbeing detection

Create a monitoring pipeline that processes smartwatch PPG, accelerometer and HRV data to classify stress and recommend relaxation strategies.

Objective 04 · Member 4

Mentorship matchmaking

Engineer a mentor–mentee matching engine using semantic profile embeddings, with automated CV extraction and credential verification.

05 — Methodology

Four phases, applied across every component.

Phase 01

Data collection & preprocessing

UGC admission cut-offs for 25 districts; 1,000+ Sri Lankan IT vacancies crawled from TopJobs and Rooster with Selenium and BeautifulSoup; wearable PPG / accelerometer datasets cleaned with band-pass filtering; mentor profile datasets.

Phase 02

Feature engineering

spaCy (en_core_web_sm) pipelines and a 150+ skill lexicon; TF-IDF with sublinear scaling and n-grams; time- and frequency-domain HRV features; CV text vectorisation; augmentation for rare career classes.

Phase 03

Modelling

Rule engine + Random Forest (C1); stacking ensemble, SGI formula and Neo4j pivot graph (C2); multi-class stress classifier (C3); Sentence-BERT cosine matching with CV verification (C4).

Phase 04

Integration & evaluation

Four microservices (ports 8006, 8008, 8001, 5000) unified through a React web portal and mobile app. Evaluated with 5-fold cross-validation, confusion matrices, latency tests and System Usability Scale surveys.
